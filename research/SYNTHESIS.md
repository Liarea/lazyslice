# Synthesis: what the research changes

Written 2026-09-05 against the seven documents in `research/` and `NAME.md`. Each fact links to the document, heading and line it came from (line numbers are the file's own, as of this date). Findings are stated as the research states them, not softened. The project was renamed **lazyslice** by [ADR-000](../docs/adr/000-name.md) on 2026-09-05; the research prose says "lazysnap" because it was written under that name.

## 1. The ten facts that most change what we build

**1. Both companies that built this product died of demand, not execution — and the people with budgets rejected the hosted model out loud.** Snaplet: "we have not reached the necessary adoption levels" ([POSTMORTEMS.md §7, L582](POSTMORTEMS.md#7-what-the-founders-said-about-why)). Neosync, a year after a face-saving acquisition: "In reality there just wasn't enough demand" ([POSTMORTEMS.md §7, L665–673](POSTMORTEMS.md#7-what-the-founders-said-about-why)). On Neosync's own launch thread a prospective user wrote "If the original data needs to be exported and sent to your Cloud for anonymization, it defeats the entire purpose… I can't say that I trust your business model", and the founder conceded the paying segment was the one *least* worried about data leaving ([POSTMORTEMS.md §3, L200–213](POSTMORTEMS.md#3-what-users-abandoned-them-over)). Neosync's cloud was priced five different ways in twelve months and the free tier was removed before the end ([POSTMORTEMS.md §6, L546–558](POSTMORTEMS.md#6-pricing-what-they-tried-and-how-it-changed)). What changes: the hosted service in BUILD_PLAN Phase 8 is the exact shape that failed twice; the research's own conclusion is "be a tool, not a company: one binary, no control plane, nothing that stops working when someone stops paying a hosting bill" ([COMPETITORS.md "The category kills companies", L657](COMPETITORS.md#8-the-category-kills-companies)). The bar we must clear is only "the pain is worse than the tool", which is lower than the bar a startup had ([POSTMORTEMS.md L673](POSTMORTEMS.md#7-what-the-founders-said-about-why)).

**2. The subset blows up through child edges from parents, and the field's answer is to hand the user a graph-pruning session; nobody shows the plan before running.** Tonic: 5% of one table became "approximately 36% of total row count"; Jailer's FAQ opens with "Why am I getting so much data back?"; condenser "greedily grabs as many rows as it can" ([COMPETITORS.md "What nobody does well" §3, L603–613](COMPETITORS.md#3-the-slice-blows-up-and-the-tools-tell-you-afterwards)). Jailer #126 shows the same over-collection is a privacy bug — customer 1's slice contained customers 2–10's orders, and it took eight months to fix ([COMPLAINTS.md FK-14, L142–146](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-1--broken-foreign-keys-and-referential-integrity)). The algorithm that keeps size predictable is a monotone worklist where every queue entry is tagged `CHILD_OK` or `PARENT_ONLY` and the child step runs only for `CHILD_OK` rows; a row first reached as a parent is never re-expanded ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §1.1, L50–77](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#11-simplest-correct-approach-a-monotone-worklist); rule restated at [L124](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)). "Percent is a lie" — plan in row counts and print the estimate ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §1.3, L139](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#13-traps)). What changes: the planner's contract is not "children with caps" but "children only from the root's own descendants, capped and depth-limited, with a row budget and a client-memory budget, printed before extraction". The plan preview with an honest estimate is the one differentiator no shipping tool has ([COMPETITORS.md L679, L703](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

**3. Silent success is the most-evidenced failure class in the corpus, and CONCEPT.md verifies only half of it.** Seven independent reports across five tools where the tool exited zero and had not done its job: no transformation applied because of dump format (Replibyte #269, still open), parallel masking reporting success with cleartext left behind, a 400-byte empty output after twenty minutes, sequences silently set to 1 by a read-only role ([COMPLAINTS.md "Frequency ranking", L754](COMPLAINTS.md#frequency-ranking); [PII-2 L204–216](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-2--leaked-pii-and-masking-that-silently-does-nothing); [TR-15 L477–481](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-4--trust-abandonment-silent-success-dread-and-vetoes)). Snaplet's transform mode *defaulted to `unsafe`* — "columns not specified in the config is simply copied over as is" — on the same screen where its own transcript shows the detector failing ([POSTMORTEMS.md §5, L417–429](POSTMORTEMS.md#5-where-complexity-accumulated); [§10 item 1, L734–735](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)). The single highest-signal complaint is longitudinal: the config was right when written and wrong three months later ([COMPLAINTS.md PII-1, L200–202](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-2--leaked-pii-and-masking-that-silently-does-nothing); [PII-6, L236](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-2--leaked-pii-and-masking-that-silently-does-nothing)). CONCEPT.md verifies foreign keys and nothing else; the research names the gaps as "verification of masking as distinct from verification of foreign keys", "silent success as a class", and "schema drift over time — if `lazysnap.yml` is committed and used in CI, the committed file *is* the stale config" ([COMPLAINTS.md "Not addressed", L823–827](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed)). What changes: no unsafe mode exists at all; a column the committed config has never seen is masked or the run stops; the run ends with assertions (FKs, no residual source values in masked columns, sequences sane, row counts non-zero where planned) and any failure is a non-zero exit.

**4. Deterministic masking is a keyed PRF, the key lives outside the committed file, and the masker should be a separate dependency-free library because it is the only part of Snaplet that survived.** Design: 32 random bytes per project, HKDF per semantic category, HMAC-SHA256 of the canonicalised value, generator driven by the hash; never a seeded global faker; GDPR treats an unkeyed hash of a low-entropy value as a lookup table waiting to be built ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.1, L146–169](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#21-simplest-correct-approach-keyed-hash-to-seed-per-semantic-category)). Canonicalise before hashing and propagate the category along FK edges or the join breaks ([L171–173](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#21-simplest-correct-approach-keyed-hash-to-seed-per-semantic-category)). A keyed hash is not a permutation: under a unique index use a ≥2⁶⁴ domain, never retry with a counter (it makes the mapping order-dependent), and refuse by name a column whose type cannot hold a large enough domain ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.2, L189–201](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#22-refinements)). Copycat — "like faker.js, but deterministic" — does 121,478 weekly downloads two years after the company died, 12× the snapshot tool it served, on a codebase last touched January 2025 ([POSTMORTEMS.md §2, L104–128](POSTMORTEMS.md#2-what-users-loved); [§11 item 1, L753–754](POSTMORTEMS.md#11-three-things-we-must-copy)). Brume already ships cross-run determinism, so this is table stakes, not a differentiator ([COMPETITORS.md L649, L684](COMPETITORS.md#7-determinism-is-an-afterthought)). Unresolved: one global key forever makes the erasure problem worse; per-entity keys with crypto-shredding are the alternative ([COMPLAINTS.md L855](COMPLAINTS.md#questions-the-brief-asked-that-the-themes-above-dont-answer-directly)).

**5. The load path is the verification: create FKs post-data, run the whole extract against one exported snapshot, reset sequences after commit, and promise bounded memory.** Because the tool owns the target schema it can use pg_dump's pre-data/data/post-data split; with FKs created last, "the `ALTER TABLE ADD FOREIGN KEY` at the end *is* the integrity verification", and `NOT VALID` + `VALIDATE CONSTRAINT` gives a per-constraint report instead of a hard stop ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §4.2, L305](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#42-refinements)). `session_replication_role = replica` and `--disable-triggers` need superuser and managed Postgres withholds both ([L307](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#42-refinements)). Key selection and extraction over minutes against a live database race unless every source connection runs `SET TRANSACTION SNAPSHOT` from one `pg_export_snapshot()` under `REPEATABLE READ` ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §4.1, L287](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#41-simplest-correct-approach)); the cost is a pinned xmin horizon on the source and, on a read replica, cancellation after `max_standby_streaming_delay` (default 30 s) — which is the shape "point it at production" usually takes ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §4.3, L323](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#43-traps)). Sequences: `setval` after commit, and `coalesce(max(id), 0)` is an error on an empty table ([L297](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#41-simplest-correct-approach)); condenser #14 has left targets with sequences at 1 since 2019 ([COMPETITORS.md L266–268](COMPETITORS.md#condenser)). Memory, not time, kills runs: 200 MB source, 3 GB RSS, OOM; 250 MB source, 51 GB ([COMPLAINTS.md SP-3/SP-4, L513–521](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-5--speed-and-resource-cost); [L817](COMPLAINTS.md#partly-addressed-with-gaps)). Selected-key sets need a byte budget the plan prints and refuses to exceed ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L126](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).

**6. Classification is names + types + validated samples + a reason string, biased to recall; ML is not the gate and a cloud model over production values contradicts the compliance story; free text and JSON are masked whole.** piicatcher's regexes match `mailing_list_id` and `passenger_count`; validators (Luhn, mod-97, `ParseAddress`, libphonenumber) plus `TABLESAMPLE` (never `LIMIT 100`, which returns the oldest rows) make the reason credible ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.1, L218–226](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#31-simplest-correct-approach-names-plus-types-plus-validated-samples-with-a-reason-string); [§3.3 L270](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#33-traps)). Column-level detectors average macro-F1 0.61–0.87 and the authors say false-negative rates "are currently still too high for practical applications"; the signal ML adds is context, captured by a cheap rule (any high-confidence PII column raises every ambiguous column in that table) ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.2, L256–263](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)). JSON: mask hit keys at every path or replace the document with `{}`; free text cannot be classified reliably from samples, so classify by name plus NER and replace the whole value ([L254](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)). CONCEPT.md's non-goals exclude binary blobs and are silent on text and JSONB; the research calls that silence "a gap, not a decision" ([COMPLAINTS.md L815](COMPLAINTS.md#partly-addressed-with-gaps)). Detection itself is now table stakes — Basecut, dbslice, fixturize and PostgreSQL Anonymizer all do it; explaining *why* is the open ground ([COMPETITORS.md L683](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

**7. "Never holds write access to the source" is a precondition we cannot enforce, only check and print; and the target is where the catastrophic failure lives.** The field almost never answers the write-access question; pg_sample requires it and fails on read replicas, condenser creates a temp database on the MySQL source ([COMPETITORS.md Orientation, L40](COMPETITORS.md#orientation)). A read-only role can itself produce a silently wrong target (TR-15, sequences at 1 because the role lacked sequence privileges, and the loud failure was lost to a PostgreSQL 18 change) ([COMPLAINTS.md L807–809](COMPLAINTS.md#directly-addressed)). `default_transaction_read_only` is "discipline wearing a server's uniform": any session can turn it off; the actual guarantee needs a role without write grants, which we verify with `has_table_privilege` and print ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.2, L564–567](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking)). The asymmetry that makes discovery safe: "A wrong source produces a useless snapshot and wastes a minute. A wrong target destroys data. So the source may be guessed; the target may only be *qualified*" — eligible only if every user table has zero rows or it carries our marker table ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.2, L562, L583](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking)). Replit's agent deleting a production database during a code freeze is the reminder that this rule must be a read-only connection *and* an invariant test, not an intention ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §5 item 4, L1084–1094](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#5-practices-to-avoid-and-the-failure-each-caused)).

**8. The zero-config first run has a precise shape, and BUILD_PLAN's sketch of it is wrong in two places.** The one-question rule, stated exactly: at most one blocking question per run, the first undetermined rung of source → target → root table; source is never asked (no safe default; the run stops with the ladder it walked and a command); target is asked only when Docker can start one; headless runs ask nothing and anything that would create or destroy something becomes a hard failure naming its flag ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.3, L585–597](SQLIT_STUDY.md#53-the-one-question-rule-stated-exactly); [§6 L801–829](SQLIT_STUDY.md#6-the-complete-question-catalogue)). Go's `client.FromEnv` does not resolve Docker contexts, so without our own resolution "every OrbStack and Colima user sees 'Docker not running' on their first run" — "the highest-risk single defect in the first-run path" ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §2.1, L133](SQLIT_STUDY.md#21-docker-container-detection--the-code-path); [§5.1, L539](SQLIT_STUDY.md#51-the-discovery-ladder)). `docker-compose.yml` is a naming source only, never parsed for a DSN — variable substitution and profiles mean the file does not describe what is running; BUILD_PLAN's ADR-006 prompt says the opposite, and its "most incoming FKs" root default promotes lookup tables ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.10, L787–797](SQLIT_STUDY.md#510-divergences-from-docsbuild_planmd)). Auto-detection that yields a connection string that does not connect is worse than none: use the address family Docker actually published ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §2.1, L236–238](SQLIT_STUDY.md#21-docker-container-detection--the-code-path)). No competitor discovers containers and proposes a source/target pair; DBSnapper's `pgdocker://` is the nearest prior art ([COMPETITORS.md L701](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

**9. Installation is the top-voted feature request in the category, native dependencies and runtime calls are what killed the dead tools' afterlives, and the licence has to be OSI-approved or `brew install` without a tap does not work.** pg_sample's most-upvoted issue is "How do I install this?" (open since 2020); Snaplet Snapshot's top two issues are install failures on Apple Silicon; Replibyte cannot be built ([COMPETITORS.md "What nobody does well" §4, L615–622](COMPETITORS.md#4-installation-is-the-top-voted-feature-request-in-this-category)). Snapshot was cut off while still growing because a `better-sqlite3` pin broke on Node 22 and a one-line fix sat unmerged for six months; a tool with a hosted LLM call degraded into a nag banner ([POSTMORTEMS.md §8, L710–712](POSTMORTEMS.md#8-the-afterlife-what-open-sourcing-actually-bought); [§10 item 5, L746–747](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)). The rule: no runtime call to anything we operate — no telemetry gate, model call, licence check, registry, shortlink — so the binary behaves identically in five years with us gone. Apache-2.0 for the CLI, DCO not CLA, one repo, no `ee/` directory; BUSL is "not an Open Source license" per SPDX and homebrew-core requires DFSG compatibility, which "directly contradicts the 'one static binary, one-line install' principle" ([LICENSE_DECISION.md L29](LICENSE_DECISION.md#corporate-adoption-friction); [Recommendation, L81–86](LICENSE_DECISION.md#recommendation)).

**10. Two 2026 entrants already reach a masked, referentially complete subset with zero authored config in about five minutes, the nineteen-year incumbent is building our two differentiators right now, and stars are not usage.** dbslice (MIT, Python, alpha, no `LICENSE` file, default depth 3 that silently drops parents) and Basecut (closed, account-gated, releases frozen since March) are the only tools under ten minutes; "If we require a YAML file before the first snapshot we are behind two 2026 entrants on day one" ([COMPETITORS.md "Time from install", L553–577](COMPETITORS.md#time-from-install-to-first-usable-snapshot); [L668](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)). Jailer's unreleased commits of the week this was written add per-row origin chains and automatic association discovery — "nobody ships this today" is not "nobody is building it" ([COMPETITORS.md Jailer, L229](COMPETITORS.md#jailer)). Stars and downloads disagree by orders of magnitude in both directions: dbslice 143 stars, 33 downloads/month; slice-db 6 stars, 13,633 ([COMPETITORS.md §4a, L624–633](COMPETITORS.md#4a-stars-measure-the-pitch-downloads-are-a-better-proxy-for-use-and-this-document-went-and-got-some)). A Show HN is a coin flip, not a channel: three of the studied projects grew with no meaningful HN thread ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §2, L89–93](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#2-the-projects)). The ground we can still own, on current evidence: plan preview with estimate, explaining the classification, config as output, verification as a command, container discovery ([COMPETITORS.md L699–705](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

## 2. Three risks that could kill the project

**Risk 1 — The category has no durable developer-facing business, and we build a company anyway.** Two funded, well-built products died of demand; the only survivor sells to enterprises at a median $45,525 a year ([COMPETITORS.md Tonic, L371](COMPETITORS.md#tonic-structural)); the surviving open-source pieces coast only when they have no runtime dependencies ([POSTMORTEMS.md §8, L712](POSTMORTEMS.md#8-the-afterlife-what-open-sourcing-actually-bought)). Snaplet's revenue was infrastructure resale that our non-goals already exclude, so "there is no version of Snaplet's revenue model available to us" ([POSTMORTEMS.md §6, L538](POSTMORTEMS.md#6-pricing-what-they-tried-and-how-it-changed)). *Mitigation:* decide now that v1 is a tool with no business attached and no control plane; treat BUILD_PLAN Phase 8's hosted service as unfunded until download data — not stars — shows unattended CI use; ship the deterministic masker as a separately named, importable, dependency-free module so it outlives the tool if the tool fails ([POSTMORTEMS.md §11 item 1, L753–754](POSTMORTEMS.md#11-three-things-we-must-copy)); keep the binary free of every call that would stop working if we stopped paying for it.

**Risk 2 — One silent leak, or one write to the wrong database, and trust is gone; "you can't un-leak it".** A loud contingent of the target audience already believes the category is irresponsible ([POSTMORTEMS.md §3, L215–232](POSTMORTEMS.md#3-what-users-abandoned-them-over); [COMPLAINTS.md TR-7, TR-13](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-4--trust-abandonment-silent-success-dread-and-vetoes)); most security teams veto prod data in non-prod "anonymized or not" ([COMPLAINTS.md TR-13, L467–469](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-4--trust-abandonment-silent-success-dread-and-vetoes)). The failure that ends the project is the quiet one: a run that exits zero with cleartext in the target, or a first run that writes masked rows into the database it should have read ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.0, L522](SQLIT_STUDY.md#50-the-rules-this-design-has-to-satisfy)). *Mitigation:* fail closed everywhere — no unsafe mode, the classifier's failure mode is mask more, an unseen column masks or stops; the target eligibility gate (zero rows or our marker) is a hard gate that no flag short of an explicit `--target` overrides; the run's own verification (FKs, residual-PII scan of masked columns, sequences, row counts) is the same code as the invariant suite and fails the run loudly; the "no wholesale-disable flag" rule becomes a CI grep, not a sentence ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §4 item 11, L901–909](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#4-practices-to-adopt-for-lazysnap)); and the README uses Brume's honest language — pseudonymisation, still personal data ([COMPETITORS.md L688](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

**Risk 3 — The subsetter runs out of road on a real schema, the way every predecessor did.** Neosync's maintainer called parent-by-child subsetting "definitely possible… we just haven't tackled it yet" and never did; the user left ([POSTMORTEMS.md §3, L236–251](POSTMORTEMS.md#3-what-users-abandoned-them-over)). Greenmask, the most mature open-source tool, panics on schemas with *no* cycles and is rewriting its subset engine in 2026 ([COMPLAINTS.md FK-8, FK-11, L112–132](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-1--broken-foreign-keys-and-referential-integrity)). Tables with no primary key, polymorphic `_type/_id` pairs, composite keys with partial NULLs, and 129-column tables are each a documented crash somewhere ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §1.2, L108–120](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements); [COMPLAINTS.md DB-9, L613–615](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-6--unsupported-database-platform-or-type)). *Mitigation:* the client-side `CHILD_OK`/`PARENT_ONLY` worklist with post-data FK creation, which has none of the cycle cliffs ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L104](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)); a fixture set that from day one covers no cycles, one self-cycle, a two-table cycle, an SCC with two overlapping cycles, a keyless junction table, a polymorphic pair and a 300-column table ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L140, L333](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#13-traps)); a row budget and memory budget that abort planning with a message rather than "collecting data indefinitely" ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L106](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)); and a hard rule that no second engine, transformer count, or integration ships while a Postgres subsetting issue is open ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 4, L743–744](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)).

## 3. Revised non-goals for v1

The list grew. Each addition names the evidence.

- **Synthetic data generation from a schema alone.** Kept, and now load-bearing: Snaplet drifted here and Neosync started here; both died ([POSTMORTEMS.md §4, L349, L385](POSTMORTEMS.md#4-what-the-product-became-that-it-did-not-start-as)).
- **Scheduling, retention, or sharing snapshots between people.** Kept; these were Snaplet's only billable units ([POSTMORTEMS.md §6, L533–538](POSTMORTEMS.md#6-pricing-what-they-tried-and-how-it-changed)).
- **A web UI, a hosted service, or any control plane between the developer and their database** — including a runtime call to anything we operate: telemetry, licence check, model call, update check, registry, image CDN ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 items 3 and 5](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)).
- **NoSQL and document stores.** Kept.
- **Schema migration or diffing.** Kept.
- **Databases other than PostgreSQL.** Kept, with a testable condition: a second engine waits until the invariant suite passes on every Postgres fixture and the engine has its own CI job ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §4 item 8, L873–880](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#4-practices-to-adopt-for-lazysnap)); Greenmask's MySQL epic has been open two years ([COMPLAINTS.md DB-4, L587–589](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-6--unsupported-database-platform-or-type)).
- **Detecting personal data inside binary blobs.** Kept, with the silence closed: text and JSON columns are not exempt; they are classified and masked whole ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.2, L254](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)).
- **New: any unsafe, pass-through, or "copy unclassified columns as-is" mode.** Not a flag we refuse — a mode that does not exist ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 1](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)).
- **New: anonymisation, k-anonymity, or any compliance guarantee.** Deterministic masking preserves frequency; the output is pseudonymised personal data under GDPR Art. 4(5) ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.3, L208](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#23-traps); [COMPETITORS.md L688](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).
- **New: a cloud or LLM classifier over production values, or any ML model as the gate.** ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.2, L263](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)).
- **New: format-preserving encryption.** The standard is in flux and no maintained Go library exists ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.2, L179–185](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#22-refinements)).
- **New: partial redaction inside free text or JSON.** Whole-value replacement only ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L254](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)).
- **New: percent-based sampling, and traversal tuning knobs exposed as configuration** (`maxCyclesLoop`, `traversalMode`, and kin). The plan decides and prints; the yml records ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 2, L737–738](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy); [COMPETITORS.md L674](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).
- **New: parsing `docker-compose.yml` for connection strings.** Naming source only ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.1, L541](SQLIT_STUDY.md#51-the-discovery-ladder)).
- **New: multi-terabyte sources and resumable extracts.** A v1 run finishes in minutes or aborts cleanly on its row and memory budget; thirty-hour Jailer runs lost to one dropped connection are a v2 problem stated, not solved ([COMPLAINTS.md SP-6, L527–531](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-5--speed-and-resource-cost)).
- **New: native compiled dependencies or cgo in the shipped binary.** ([POSTMORTEMS.md §3, L277–285](POSTMORTEMS.md#3-what-users-abandoned-them-over)).
- **New: a CLA, an `ee/` directory, or a source-available licence.** Apache-2.0, DCO, one repo ([LICENSE_DECISION.md Recommendation, L81–86](LICENSE_DECISION.md#recommendation)).
- **New: an auditable sign-off artefact for compliance.** Wanted (TR-10 "Sounds fantastic"), out of scope for v1, tracker entry ([COMPLAINTS.md L833](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed)).

## 4. CONCEPT.md statements the research contradicts or weakens

Each entry: the quote, then what the research says instead. These are applied to CONCEPT.md alongside this document.

1. **"# lazysnap"** — Contradicted. A Go TUI in the same lazy family is live in the Go module proxy under this name; npm, the GitHub account and `.com` are taken; DBSnapper and Snaplet own "snap" in this exact category ([NAME.md §1, L76–89](../NAME.md#1-lazysnap-collision-audit); [§4, L406–423](../NAME.md#4-recommendation-and-reasoning)). [ADR-000](../docs/adr/000-name.md) renamed the project lazyslice; every `lazysnap.yml` becomes `lazyslice.yml`.

2. **"a small, referentially complete, anonymised copy"** — Contradicted. Deterministic masking preserves frequency and is pseudonymisation under GDPR Art. 4(5), not anonymisation; "pseudonymised information constitutes personal data under the GDPR" ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.1, L167](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#21-simplest-correct-approach-keyed-hash-to-seed-per-semantic-category); [§2.3, L208](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#23-traps)). Brume's README is the model for correct language; "we must not claim more than we deliver" ([COMPETITORS.md L688](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)). Replace "anonymised" with "pseudonymised".

3. **"First run asks at most one question and then works."** — Weakened. True on the happy path only; Scenario C asks nothing and stops with a command, a secret prompt does not count, and a headless run asks nothing and hard-fails on anything that would create or destroy ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.3, L587–597](SQLIT_STUDY.md#53-the-one-question-rule-stated-exactly)). Say "at most one blocking question; headless runs ask none".

4. **"Configuration is emitted after a run as a record of what happened, never demanded before it."** — Weakened. Once `lazysnap.yml` is committed and used in CI "the committed file *is* the stale config and drift returns"; there is no stated behaviour for a column the config has never seen, and the safe answer — mask it, fail loudly rather than pass it through — "is not written down anywhere. This remains the most important gap" ([COMPLAINTS.md L827](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed); [HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.3, L269](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#33-traps)). Add the fail-closed rule.

5. **"Anything that might be personal data is masked unless the user opts a column out, and the tool explains why it masked each one."** — Weakened in two places. Column-name classification cannot catch PII in `notes` or JSONB; the tool must mask free text and JSON whole and say when it could not classify a column ([COMPLAINTS.md L815](COMPLAINTS.md#partly-addressed-with-gaps); [HARD_PROBLEMS.md L254](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)). And the classifier's failure mode must be "mask more, never pass through" — Snaplet's detector failed in its own documented transcript while the default was pass-through ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 1, L735](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)).

6. **"It never holds write access to the source."** — Weakened. "Read-only is a precondition, not a guarantee": a least-privilege role produced a silently wrong target (TR-15), and a session-level read-only setting is not access control — the tool can only check the role's privileges and print what it found ([COMPLAINTS.md L807–809](COMPLAINTS.md#directly-addressed); [SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.2, L564–567](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking)). The principle also says nothing about the target, where the catastrophic write lives ([SQLIT_STUDY.md L583](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking)). Restate as "opens the source read-only, checks the role and says what it found; writes only to an empty target or one it wrote before".

7. **"We refuse to ship a flag that disables masking wholesale."** — Weakened by being too narrow. The research says forbid the *mode*, not just the flag: "There should be no `unsafe`: only 'masked', and 'explicitly opted out, per column, recorded'" ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 1, L735](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)), and make the rule a failing CI test rather than a sentence ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §4 item 11, L908–909](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#4-practices-to-adopt-for-lazysnap)).

8. **"One static binary, one-line install, and the same command a human types works headless in CI."** — Weakened by omission. Supported as far as it goes, but the durability lesson is stronger: no native dependency and no runtime call to anything we operate, so the binary works in five years with us gone ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 items 3 and 5, L741, L747](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)). Also, "works in CI" is asserted by every tool and demonstrated by none; treat it as a hypothesis to prove with a published CI recipe and cost ([COMPETITORS.md L670](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)).

9. **"1. Introspects the source schema: tables, keys, foreign keys, samples."** — Weakened. Missing the things that break loads: unique indexes including expression and partial ones, sequences and identity columns, partitions, enums, generated columns, row-level security ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §2.2 L193, §4.1 L295–299, §4.3 L332](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#42-refinements)). Sampling must be `TABLESAMPLE` or `pg_stats`, never head-of-table `LIMIT` ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §3.3, L270](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#33-traps)).

10. **"3. Plans a subset: a root table, a row count, parents to completeness, children with caps, cycles handled."** — Weakened. "Children with caps" is not the size control; the control is following child edges only from rows reached by child edges, plus a depth limit, a row budget and a memory budget ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §1.1 L50–77; §1.2 L124, L126](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#11-simplest-correct-approach-a-monotone-worklist)). "Cycles handled" must include zero cycles (Greenmask panics on their absence) and must print which edge was deferred ([COMPLAINTS.md FK-8, L112–118](COMPLAINTS.md#theme-1--broken-foreign-keys-and-referential-integrity); [COMPETITORS.md L676](COMPETITORS.md#what-we-must-match-to-be-credible)). Lookup tables are copied whole; tables without a primary key need a stated fallback; polymorphic pairs are inferred and reported ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L90, L110–116, L120](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)). The plan and its estimate must be printed before extraction — the one thing no tool does ([COMPETITORS.md L613, L679](COMPETITORS.md#3-the-slice-blows-up-and-the-tools-tell-you-afterwards)).

11. **"4. Streams the rows out, masks them deterministically so joins still work, and loads them into the target."** — Weakened. "Deterministically" does not say across runs, which is the property that makes a snapshot diffable and a bug reproducible; say which is promised and where the key lives ([COMPLAINTS.md L813](COMPLAINTS.md#partly-addressed-with-gaps); [HARD_PROBLEMS.md L169](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#21-simplest-correct-approach-keyed-hash-to-seed-per-semantic-category)). "Streams" implies constant memory but nothing promises it, and memory is what kills these runs ([COMPLAINTS.md L817](COMPLAINTS.md#partly-addressed-with-gaps)). The extract must read one exported snapshot or the plan and the extract race ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md §4.1, L287](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#41-simplest-correct-approach)).

12. **"5. Verifies foreign-key integrity in the target and writes `lazysnap.yml` so the run is reproducible."** — Weakened. FK verification is "the loud half"; nothing verifies that no cleartext reached the target, that sequences are sane, or that planned tables are non-empty — four tools shipped masking that silently no-opped, and a residual-PII scan is "table stakes, not a differentiator" ([COMPLAINTS.md L823–825](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed)). Reproducibility needs more than the yml: the key fingerprint, the tool version and the source snapshot id ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L118](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).

13. **Transcript: "source: shop-db   target: shop-db-test"** — Weakened. Auto-detecting what to read and what to write is a conditional-state decision; sqlit's own rule demands each be printed with its provenance and the flag that changes it ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §3.4, L473](SQLIT_STUDY.md#34-contributor-docs); [§5.2, L569–574](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking)).

14. **Transcript: "✓ 500 customers and everything they touch, in 38s"** — Contradicted on "everything they touch": children are capped and parents pulled for completeness are leaves, so the slice is deliberately *not* everything ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L124](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)). "38s" and "500" are unanchored to any stated user request; the research calls them "a design target chosen for the pitch, not a number this research supports" ([COMPLAINTS.md L845](COMPLAINTS.md#questions-the-brief-asked-that-the-themes-above-dont-answer-directly)).

15. **Transcript: "✓ foreign keys verified · wrote lazysnap.yml"** — Weakened, per item 12: the success line must also report that masked columns hold no source values and that sequences were reset ([COMPLAINTS.md L823–825, L835](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed)).

16. **Non-goal: "Databases other than PostgreSQL. MySQL, SQLite and SQL Server come after the Postgres path is excellent."** — Weakened. "Both companies wrote that sentence and then did not obey it"; the test is whether we say no to the fifteenth transformer and the Slack integration while a Postgres subsetting issue is open ([POSTMORTEMS.md §10 item 4, L743–744](POSTMORTEMS.md#10-five-things-we-will-not-copy)). Make the condition testable.

17. **Non-goal: "Detecting personal data inside binary blobs."** — Weakened by what it leaves out; see item 5. Text and JSONB must be stated as in scope and masked whole.

18. **Non-goal list as a whole** — Incomplete. Missing: no unsafe mode, no anonymisation claim, no cloud classifier, no FPE, no traversal knobs, no compose-YAML parsing, no native dependencies, no runtime call to us (section 3 above). BUILD_PLAN's Gate 1 requires that non-goals "grew, not shrank".

Unchanged by the research and worth saying so: the user, the moment they reach for it, "Terminal first" as a principle, "Classifies columns … and says why" (the one detection feature nobody else has), and "Documentation is never the fix for a confusing first run".

## 5. Questions phase 2 architecture must take a position on

1. **Language (ADR-001).** Go is still the answer for the single binary and pgx's COPY, but Go's Docker client does not resolve contexts and no maintained Go FPE library exists — who writes context resolution, and is FPE struck for v1? ([SQLIT_STUDY.md L133, L539](SQLIT_STUDY.md#51-the-discovery-ladder); [HARD_PROBLEMS.md L183](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#22-refinements))
2. **Subsetting engine (ADR-002).** Client-side monotone worklist with `CHILD_OK`/`PARENT_ONLY` provenance, or SQL pushdown with recursive CTEs on the source as Greenmask does? The research argues the worklist, because the tool must print why a row is present and must not write temp tables on the source ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L126](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).
3. **Row mode is decided once.** A row first reached as a parent is never re-expanded as a child even if a later child edge would reach it. Accept that as the size control, or specify the larger "most permissive mode wins" algorithm? ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L77](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#11-simplest-correct-approach-a-monotone-worklist))
4. **Load path and the target's existing schema.** Recreate the target schema and create FKs post-data (verification = FK creation succeeds), or load into an already-migrated target with deferred constraints and NULL-then-UPDATE? What happens when the target has migrations applied but no rows — the common compose test database? ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L305–307](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#42-refinements); [SQLIT_STUDY.md L562](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking))
5. **Row identity without a primary key.** The fallback chain (PK → unique index → inferred pseudo-key → `ctid` under the held snapshot) and the size threshold at which a `ctid`-only table is refused ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L110–116](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).
6. **Determinism scope and key management.** Within a run, across runs with the same key, or per-entity keys with crypto-shredding? Where the key lives (`lazyslice.secret`, env var, OS keyring), what the yml records (fingerprint only), and what a CI run without the key gets ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L169](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#21-simplest-correct-approach-keyed-hash-to-seed-per-semantic-category); [COMPLAINTS.md L855](COMPLAINTS.md#questions-the-brief-asked-that-the-themes-above-dont-answer-directly)).
7. **Unique-index collisions.** Adopt the rule: ≥2⁶⁴ domain with a hash-derived suffix, no retry-with-counter, refuse by name when the column's type cannot hold `n²/2ε`? ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L191–201](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#22-refinements))
8. **Classifier design and its failure mode.** Names + types + validated samples + dictionaries + neighbouring-column rule, no ML gate; what exactly happens on re-run when a column is not in the committed yml — mask with a default generator and warn, or stop? ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L226, L263, L269](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#33-traps))
9. **Free text and JSON.** Whole-value replacement for hit keys versus `{}` for varying-key documents; what "same-length filler" means for `text` columns the app validates ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L254](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#32-refinements)).
10. **The verification set and exit codes.** Which assertions end a run (FKs, residual-PII scan, sequences, non-zero planned tables, untransformed columns byte-identical), and the exit-code table for CI to branch on ([COMPLAINTS.md L825](COMPLAINTS.md#not-addressed); [SQLIT_STUDY.md L751–765](SQLIT_STUDY.md#57-error-presentation)).
11. **Source snapshot.** `pg_export_snapshot` under `REPEATABLE READ` on every source connection; print the estimated hold time; treat standby cancellation as an expected failure with a retry message ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L287, L323](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#43-traps)).
12. **Memory budget.** Data structure for selected keys, the resident-set estimate the plan prints, the default byte budget, and the override flag ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L126](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).
13. **Discovery ladder and the one-question rule (ADR-006).** Adopt the ladder, the target eligibility gate, the exact one-question statement and the question catalogue; compose YAML as naming only — this contradicts BUILD_PLAN's ADR-006 prompt and must be decided explicitly ([SQLIT_STUDY.md §5.1–5.3, §5.10, §6](SQLIT_STUDY.md#510-divergences-from-docsbuild_planmd)).
14. **Root-table default.** BUILD_PLAN says "most incoming FKs"; the study says inbound minus outbound with lookup filtering and a name preference, and admits the heuristic is untested ([SQLIT_STUDY.md L603–607, L841](SQLIT_STUDY.md#54-root-table-default-and-why-it-can-be-defaulted-at-all)).
15. **Flag naming.** `--take` (BUILD_PLAN and invariant I6) versus `-n`/`--rows`; resolve before I6 is written against a flag that does not exist ([SQLIT_STUDY.md L795–797](SQLIT_STUDY.md#510-divergences-from-docsbuild_planmd)).
16. **Source write access policy.** Read-only session plus privilege check, printed; warn or block on a writable role; is `--require-read-only-role` a v1 flag and exit code? ([SQLIT_STUDY.md L564–567, L760](SQLIT_STUDY.md#52-choosing-source-and-target-without-asking))
17. **Polymorphic associations.** Infer `_type/_id` and `content_type_id/object_id` pairs, map values to tables, treat as parent-direction virtual FKs and report — versus config-only as every competitor does ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L120](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#12-refinements)).
18. **The masker as a separate module.** Its name, module path, and whether it ships with its own release cadence and tests so it can outlive the tool ([POSTMORTEMS.md L753–754](POSTMORTEMS.md#11-three-things-we-must-copy)).
19. **Which CONCEPT rules become enforcement.** A CI grep for `--no-mask|--disable-mask|--skip-mask`, an invariant that the source connection issued no write, hooks denying agent commits and edits to accepted ADRs ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §4 items 7, 9, 11](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#4-practices-to-adopt-for-lazysnap)).
20. **THREAT_MODEL positions.** Pseudonymisation not anonymisation; whether real email domains are kept (employer leak) or replaced with RFC 2606 domains; frequency and prefix leaks; what the tool says about erasure requests against snapshots ([HARD_PROBLEMS.md L175, L206–209](HARD_PROBLEMS.md#23-traps)).
21. **Licence mechanics (ADR-00x or CONTRIBUTING).** Apache-2.0, `SPDX-License-Identifier` headers, no `NOTICE` unless a dependency needs it, DCO with `dco-check`, the written "will not be relicensed" promise, who holds copyright, and what licence covers the `lazyslice.yml` schema ([LICENSE_DECISION.md L81–87](LICENSE_DECISION.md#recommendation)).
22. **The hosted-service plan.** Keep BUILD_PLAN Phase 8 as written, defer it behind a download-based adoption signal, or strike it and state the project is a tool with no business attached ([POSTMORTEMS.md L673](POSTMORTEMS.md#7-what-the-founders-said-about-why); [COMPETITORS.md L657](COMPETITORS.md#8-the-category-kills-companies)).
23. **Test fixtures as the definition of the subsetter.** Commit the schema set from Risk 3 before the planner exists, built from real `information_schema` output, never from memory ([AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md §5 item 6, L1105–1110](AI_PROJECT_PRACTICES.md#5-practices-to-avoid-and-the-failure-each-caused)).
